# Review authority and limits

The user explicitly requested publication of an append-only reviewed v0.2 successor and supplied the four required study classifications and capability restrictions. This instruction authorizes this evidence-synthesis release; it does not authorize a new experiment, runtime change, capability promotion or new physiological rule.

Codex performs package integrity verification, checks the supplied conclusions against finalized reports, and records an entry-specific admission review. Reviewer fields identify that role honestly. They do **not** assert an external neuroscientist reviewed this release, that the user reviewed the generated byte hashes in advance, or that a cryptographic signature proves biological truth. Final artifacts remain available for the user's review.

The inherited v0.1 `independent_evidence` source-kind token means a newly admitted evidence artifact distinct from v0.1's sources. Here it pins completed post-v0.1 study packages/reports. It does not mean these studies are independent biological replications: the diagnostic motivates the boundary study, the empirical study reuses measurements, and repeated T5 recordings are explicitly non-independent.

Historical claims, source objects, study classifications, canonical identity and the old Stage-4 contract remain identical. The old `authorized_now: false` contract is a historical release rule, not a claim that the separately authorized Stage-4 experiment never occurred. New scoped claims record its result without rewriting that history.

The user also authorized specification-only sensory metadata and Stage-5 readiness assessment. These are additive governance sidecars referenced by the v0.2 release, not runtime operators and not new biological capability entries. Stage 5 remains unauthorized and undesigned. New physiology or capability admission requires a later explicit evidence review and separately hashed release.
